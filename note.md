# A reduction, and a negative map, for the 5→6 gap in the Hadwiger–Nelson problem

*Draft — all computational claims reproducible from the scripts in this
repository; see §6.*

## Abstract

The chromatic number of the plane is known to satisfy 5 ≤ χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7. We
observe that, by results already in the literature, the lower bound χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6
would follow from a single finite object: a unit-distance graph **B** with a
designated vertex pair at distance φ = (√5+1)/2 that receives distinct colours
in every proper 5-colouring. We report a systematic, instrument-validated
computational search establishing that B — and every weaker precursor of B we
could formulate — is absent from all currently known 5-chromatic unit-distance
graphs and from several natural construction families, including two arithmetic
worlds that have never previously been searched. We further report a small
positive observation (the field ℚ(ζ₅) contains exactly 90 unit vectors with
common denominator 11, a denser commensurable system than the one underlying
the 2018 lower-bound constructions) and two methodological findings about
instruments for this problem class. All computations use exact arithmetic in
the relevant number fields; no floating-point edge decisions occur anywhere.

## 1. The reduction

Parts [P20], following Huddleston, de Grey, and Exoo–Ismailescu, exhibits an
explicit 31-vertex graph G₃₁ in the plane, with 28 edges of length 1 and 28
edges of length φ, whose chromatic number as a two-distance graph is 6: no
5-colouring is proper for both distance classes simultaneously.

Suppose a finite unit-distance graph B exists with designated vertices u, v at
distance φ such that every proper 5-colouring of B gives u and v distinct
colours. Place a congruent copy of B on each φ-edge of G₃₁, identifying the
designated pair with the edge's endpoints. The union is a unit-distance graph
(all edges have length 1; incidental unit coincidences between copies only add
constraints). In any proper 5-colouring of the union, each copy of B forces
its φ-pair non-monochromatic, so the induced colouring of G₃₁'s vertex set is
proper for both distance classes — contradicting χ(G₃₁, {1, φ}) = 6. Hence no
proper 5-colouring exists, and χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6, with an explicit witness of roughly
31 + 28·|B| vertices.

Everything below concerns where B is not.

## 2. Methods and instrument validation

**Exact arithmetic.** Coordinates live in explicit number fields — real
multiquadratic fields ℚ(√a, √b, …), cyclotomic fields ℚ(ζₙ), and quadratic
extensions of cyclotomics — with elements represented as rational coordinate
vectors over a canonical basis. The unit-distance predicate is decided
exactly; floating point is used only to *narrow* candidate pairs, never to
decide them.

**Colouring queries.** Standard one-hot SAT encodings, solved with CaDiCaL
(via PySAT) and kissat 4.0.4. Three query types: k-colourability;
*directed pair queries* (can vertices u, v share a colour in a proper
k-colouring?); and *pattern enumeration* (how many essentially distinct local
colourings support a given pair being monochromatic, counted by solve-and-block
on the pair's neighbourhood projection).

**Validation on known results.** The stack reproduces, from the original
papers' constructions, with all published intermediate counts matching:

- de Grey's 1581-vertex graph G [dG18]: built from the 39-point seed recipe
  (39 → 397 → 791 → 1581); verified not 4-colourable (421 s UNSAT) and
  5-colourable (instant, colouring independently checked), i.e. χ(G) = 5.
- de Grey's lemma on his 121-vertex graph L (no 4-colouring avoids a
  monochromatic √3-triple in every hexagon): UNSAT in 0.09 s.
- Haugland's spindle-free pipeline [H26]: the 21-vertex heptagonal seed
  (84 arc directions, matching), T₅ = 1042, T₆ = 12856, G₁ = 740/3985, and
  G₁'s forced-pair lemma — no 4-colouring of G₁ gives its designated
  √3-pair equal colours — proven UNSAT independently by two engines
  (CaDiCaL 7 h 40 m; kissat ≤ 2 h 15 m). G₂ = 1066/6264 matches; G₃ =
  2131/12530 matches and is 5-colourable; its non-4-colourability we did not
  re-prove (our kissat run was stopped at 7h46m as a resource decision — the
  published result stands, and the exported instance ships with this
  repository for anyone who wants their own UNSAT).
- Parts' 510-vertex graph [P20b], the record's neighbourhood: parsed from
  Heule's CNP-SAT repository's exact symbolic coordinates into
  ℚ(√3, √5, √11); our edge detection independently reproduces all 2504
  published edges with no discrepancies.
- Positive control for the sampling instrument: G₁'s pair, *proven* forced,
  sampled across 150 diverse proper 4-colourings — never monochromatic.
  PASSED; the sampling-tier nulls below are gated on this control.

## 3. The negative map

Throughout, "diverse samples" means proper colourings generated by
solve-and-block with randomised assumptions (see §5 for why diversification
matters). Directed queries and pattern counts, not sampling, ground every
negative claim below.

**3.1 No near-forced pairs in any known 5-chromatic graph (k = 5).**
For the graphs of 510, 517, 529, and 553 vertices and de Grey's G (1581):
across 1.65 million vertex pairs, every non-adjacent pair is monochromatic in
some proper 5-colouring (witnessed explicitly). The stiffest pair found (in
the 510) is monochromatic in 7.5 % of samples and admits > 100 essentially
distinct supporting patterns. Being 5-chromatic confers no measurable rigidity
on 5-colourings.

**3.2 No aggregate forcing either.** The weaker property "every 5-colouring
contains *some* monochromatic pair at distance d" — the level-5 analogue of the
lemma that powers [dG18] — fails instantly (SAT) for all shelf graphs at
d = √3 (1204–3216 pairs each) and for de Grey's pentagonal G₁₂₆ at d = φ.

**3.3 Composition does not create rigidity.** Unioning copies of the 510
coupled by field-internal chord rotations about its best pivot (30 new unit
edges per copy), or welded by its own Minkowski translation structure (146 of
510 vertices shared per copy), leaves the pair-frequency distribution
statistically unchanged through 5 copies (~2000–2300 vertices).

**3.4 The two known arithmetic worlds barely intersect.** Gluing the
heptagonal arc lattice (ℚ(ζ₄₂)) to the multiquadratic world (ℚ(√3,√5,√11)) at
the natural anchor: among all cross-world point pairs (83,581 × 510 at radius
3), exactly six non-shared unit coincidences occur, and all six involve only
triangular-lattice points — zero genuinely heptagonal contact. (80-digit
verification; the two fields share only ℚ(√3), so scarcity is expected, and
observed.)

**3.5 The G₁ template does not transplant to the pentagonal world.**
Haugland's construction of forced-pair gadgets — path-sets between the
designated pair over a commensurable arc alphabet, then a density core —
yields *empty* 6-, 7-, and 8-cores in ℚ(ζ₅) for both the 34-arc height-graded
alphabet and the full 90-arc denominator-11 alphabet (§4), despite T-sets up
to 23,874 points. The pentagonal path-lattice cannot sustain the density the
template requires.

**3.6 Minkowski depth does not approach forcing.** Powers of the unit
pentagon G₅^⊕n up to n = 13 (2380 vertices) show minimum φ-pair
mono-frequency falling monotonically with n — but every apparent near-forced
pair has ≥ 50 supporting patterns and answers SAT instantly under a directed
query. The gradient is a sampling artifact (§5), not emerging rigidity.
Pairwise sums of the 90-arc system (4141 points) are likewise thin: mean
degree ≈ 4 and 4-colourable.

## 4. A small positive observation, and a question

Solving the norm equation |w|² = D² over w ∈ ℤ[ζ₅], the number of unit
vectors with common denominator D is 10 (the trivial ±ζ₅ᵏ) for every D we
tested except D = 11 and D = 31, which each carry exactly **90**. For
comparison, the arc system underlying [H26] comprises 84 unit vectors over
denominator 7 in ℚ(ζ₄₂) — and that system's coincidence-rich path lattice is
precisely what makes the level-4 forcing constructions dense enough to work.

The 90-arc system over 11, despite being nominally denser, produces thin
lattices (§3.5–3.6). **Question:** what property of the denominator-7
heptagonal system produces its unit-coincidence density, and which
(field, denominator) pairs share it? An answer would say where — if anywhere —
a B-hunt by lattice growth could succeed, replacing blind search with
arithmetic.

## 5. Instrument notes

**Sampling frequency conflates "forbidden" with "unvisited".** As graphs grow,
solve-and-block sampling covers a vanishing fraction of colouring space; pairs
can read as never-monochromatic across 1000 diverse samples yet answer SAT in
milliseconds when asked directly. Monotone "stiffening" trends across a graph
family can be pure sampling bias (§3.6 fell to exactly this). Directed queries
and pattern counts are the only trustworthy measures; frequency is at most a
tie-breaker at fixed size. Relatedly, undiversified solve-and-block returns
near-clone colourings: on the 510, 40 naive samples witnessed 21 % of pair
comonochromies, while 40 assumption-diversified samples witnessed 97 %.

**Single-proof parallelism does not pay here.** On the hardest instance we
hold (G₁'s forced-pair lemma, 57k-clause UNSAT), cube-and-conquer with
march_cu (depth-capped; unbounded lookahead stalls) gave ≈ 1.2× wall-clock at
≈ 5× worse core-efficiency with per-cube kissat, and no improvement with
persistent incremental iglucose workers. For this instance class: one fast
core under kissat per proof; spend parallelism across independent instances,
where it is free.
