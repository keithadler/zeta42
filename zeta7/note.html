Research note · draft · September 2026

The ζ(7) Barrier

Numerical evidence that the Hankel-determinant method behind the recent irrationality proof of ζ(5) does not extend to ζ(7), and a measurement of by how much.

Computations with exact rational arithmetic (python-flint) and certified ball arithmetic. Code: keithadler/zeta42, directory zeta7/.
Summary

A. Fauzan's preprint ζ(5) is irrational (17 September 2026; Lean formalisation at mo271/zeta5) builds integer polynomials \(P_K\) of degree linear in \(n\) from Hankel determinants of a positive moment functional, and shows \(0 < P_K(\zeta(5)) \le e^{-cn^2}\). We generalise the construction to \(\zeta(s)\) for odd \(s\) and measure \(\log P_K(\zeta(s))\) exactly, using the primitive part of each determinant, which is the best normalisation any proof could reach.

The method gives \(-0.95\), \(-0.01\), \(+0.97\), \(+1.86\) (units of \(K^2\)) for \(s = 3, 5, 7, 9\) in its plain form. It works easily for ζ(3), barely for ζ(5), and misses ζ(7) by roughly one step. Across about fifteen variations, including a complete description of the admissible measures, no ζ(7) configuration comes out negative. The excess comes from the denominators of \(H_j^{(7)}\) and is spread evenly over all prime ranges.

1 The construction for general odd s

Let \(F(y) = 1/(e^{2\pi y}-1)\) and take the weight \(w_s(y) = y^s F^{(s-1)}(y) \big/ \tfrac{(s-1)!}{2}\), which is positive on \((0,\infty)\) because \(F\) is completely monotone. Writing \(t = y^2\), its moment functional satisfies

\[ \mu(t^k) = (-1)^k B_{2k+2}\,\frac{(2k+s)!}{(2k+2)!\,(s-1)!}, \qquad \mu\!\left(\frac{1}{t+j^2}\right) = j^{s-1}\bigl(\zeta(s) - H_j^{(s)}\bigr) - \frac{1}{s-1} + \frac{1}{2j}. \]

For \(s = 5\) this is the paper's Hermite formula, with constant \(-\tfrac14 + \tfrac{1}{2j}\). For \(s = 3, 7\) we identified the constant \(-\tfrac{1}{s-1} + \tfrac{1}{2j}\) numerically to 40 digits; it agrees with Hermite's formula for the Hurwitz zeta function, which also holds at non-integer \(a = j/2\).

Replacing \(\zeta(s)\) by a variable \(X\) gives \(\mu_X\). For a rational function \(R(t) = W(t)/D_K(t)\), with \(D_m(t) = \prod_{j\le m}(t+j^2)\), form the Hankel matrix \(G_{ab} = \mu_X(t^{a+b}R)\), where \(a, b < h\), and set \(\Delta(X) = \det G\). Then \(\Delta(\zeta(s)) > 0\) as a Gram determinant, \(\deg \Delta \le \#\text{poles}\), and \(P_K\) is the primitive part of \(\Delta\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[X]\). Since the degree grows linearly, \(\zeta(s)\) is irrational if \(\log P_K(\zeta(s)) \le -c\,n^2\). The paper takes \(K = 40n\), \(N = 3n\), \(W = D_N^6\), \(h = 37n\).

Validation. The code reproduces the paper's value \(\log P_{40}(\zeta(5)) = -265.13\) exactly, using two independent implementations.

3 Where the ζ(7) excess comes from

The harmonic sums. As a diagnostic (not a valid construction), setting \(H_j^{(s)} := 0\) lowers the arithmetic cost by \(1.21K^2\) for \(s = 5\) and by \(2.03K^2\) for \(s = 7\). The difference, 0.82, is nearly the whole gap between the two.

4 Structural facts about the family

  • Every admissible measure is known. The measure \(w_s(y)R(y^2)\) is positive exactly when the numerator satisfies \(N(y^2) = |\Pi(iy)|^2\) for a real polynomial \(\Pi\) (Fejér–Riesz / Markov–Lukács). Residues are then \(c_j \propto \Pi(j)\Pi(-j)/D'(-j^2)\). Integer roots of \(\Pi\) give the product numerators \(\prod(t+i^2)^{e_i}\). Root 0 gives the factor \(t^m\), which is the weight \(y^{s+2m}F^{(s-1)}\).
  • The Bernoulli part cannot be avoided. The coefficient of \(X\) in \(\mu_X(R)\) is \(\sum_j \operatorname{res}_j\, j^{s-1}\), and it vanishes when \(R = O(t^{-(s+3)/2})\), which follows from expanding \(R\) at infinity. So \(X\) appears only if \(R\) keeps high-degree mass, which forces the polynomial part and its Bernoulli moments.
  • The ζ(s) part is a Beukers-type integral. \(\sum_j \rho_j j^{s-1}(\zeta(s) - H_j^{(s)}) = \frac{1}{(s-1)!}\int_0^1 (-\log z)^{s-1}\frac{\sum_j \rho_j j^{s-1} z^j}{1-z}\,dz\), so the arithmetic depends only on the residues.
  • Other one-dimensional Gram structures reduce to the measure. A Gram matrix of \(p_a/Q\) against \(\mu\) is the polynomial Gram against \(\mu/Q^2\). Positive point masses only enlarge the determinant. Positive combinations of constructions are at least as large as each term, and signed ones lose positivity.
  • The determinants are irreducible. \(P_K\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Z}\) in every case tested, including the paper's, so no smaller factor can be split off.

6 What would be needed

Within this framework, a proof for ζ(7) needs about \(0.8\,h^2\) of extra \(p\)-adic cancellation in the sums \(\sum_j c_j\, j^6 H_j^{(7)}\), spread across all prime ranges, or an analytic gain of the same size. Neither is available from any admissible measure for the Hermite weight. The questions we cannot answer:

  • Is there a positive measure, possibly on \((0,\infty)^2\) and not a product, whose moments are linear in ζ(7) alone and whose arithmetic avoids the full \(d_K^7\)?
  • Does a well-poised symmetry in the style of Ball–Rivoal and Zudilin have an analogue for these Hankel determinants, giving savings on \(H_j^{(7)}\) denominators?
  • Can the analytic side be made to improve with \(s\), as it does for Ball–Rivoal linear forms, while keeping a single constant?

Any candidate can be scored in minutes with the code in zeta7/.

8 Caveats

All results are numerical, at \(K \le 80\). They measure the exact content of each determinant, so a positive score is an obstruction for that construction at that size, but they are not an asymptotic proof in either direction. The \(s = 7\) Hermite formula was identified numerically to 40 digits. The hill-climb covers block profiles with 8 blocks, not every exponent sequence.
